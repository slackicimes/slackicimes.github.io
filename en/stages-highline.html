Another question?

Choosing your course

What slackline level do I need?

+–

Our “First Steps”, “Discovery Weekend” and “Youth Course” courses have no slackline prerequisite. Of course, you will go further if you can already walk on a slackline, but if you cannot, do not worry, we are here to teach you! :)

For the “Rigging” course, you need to be able to do a sit start on a slackline on the ground, to have been on a highline before, and to know how to set up a slackline on the ground or be comfortable handling ropes.

For the “Back 2 Les Arcs” course, you need to have practiced highlining already, and to master a sit start on a ground line, climbing back up the leash and getting back on the line.

Which course should I choose?

+–

Only have one weekend available? Our Discovery Weekend courses are made for you.

The First Steps courses are for beginners, whether you have never been on a line or want to progress.

Do you want to learn how to rig a highline as well as practice? The Rigging course is perfect.

Have you already taken one of our courses, or already practiced highlining? The Back 2 Les Arcs course is for you!

Are you between 10 and 18 years old? The Youth Course is designed especially for you.

What is the minimum age to join the courses?

+–

Our courses are open from 15 years old for young people coming alone, or from 10 years old if accompanied.

We are flexible about age: the most important thing is to feel comfortable in a group made up mostly of adults. To stay at the Slackcamp, participants need to be independent for meals and bedtime, or be accompanied. However, we recommend our Youth Courses, designed especially for participants under 16.

Do I need to be sporty? What if I am afraid of heights?

+–

You do not need to be an athlete, but good overall physical condition is necessary: as an example, being able to do 1 pull up or 3 push ups.

Being afraid of heights is not a problem: highlining is actually a great way to face and overcome your fears.

Can I come alone?

+–

Of course, most participants come alone!

Groups have 20 people on the 5 day courses (12 on the Rigging course) and 6 people on the weekends.

Our instructors speak French and English.

Do minors need parental authorization?

+–

Yes, participants under 18 need a parental authorization.

Registration

What does the course price include?

+–

The price includes the chairlift and all the highline equipment, except your personal harness and lanyards.

Staying at the Slackcamp is an option you choose when you register.

How does payment work?

+–

You pay when you register, on the HelloAsso platform. You will find the links on our Registrations page.

What happens if a course is cancelled?

+–

If we cancel the course, your registration fee is refunded in full.

If you cancel, you get a 90% refund when someone from our waiting list buys your place. You can also resell your place yourself. Otherwise, no refund is made.

What if the weather is bad?

+–

The weather is not a problem: thanks to the Slackcamp, we have other options and we adapt the program.

In 8 years of courses, we have never had to cancel a course because of the weather.

Am I insured during the course?

+–

Yes, participants are covered by our insurance during the highline sessions.

Course days and equipment

What does a typical week look like?

+–

A typical week is organized as follows:

  • schedule: 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.;
  • first day: meet at 9 a.m. at the Slackcamp;
  • following days: meet at the bottom of the Cachette chairlift or at the Slackcamp;
  • ground slackline and workshops at the Slackcamp, highline at the Deux Têtes spot;
  • one day at a lake on the Back 2 Les Arcs and Youth Course courses;
  • one night of bivouac at the Deux Têtes spot.

Is highlining dangerous?

+–

Safety is at the heart of our courses: you are attached to the line at all times, and the whole system is redundant.

What equipment should I bring?

+–

Collective highline equipment is provided.

We ask trainees to bring their harness and a pair of double lanyards.

You need a harness with a double tie in point: harnesses with a single one are not suitable for highlining.

For the lanyards, you have two options: a ready made double climbing lanyard (sold at Decathlon or in specialist shops), or a piece of dynamic rope (2 m, 8 to 10 mm in diameter) with 2 screwgate carabiners. We can help you tie them. Via ferrata lanyards are not suitable.

You do not need a helmet, except on the Rigging course.

What should I pack?

+–

Here are the essentials for the week, on top of your harness and lanyards:

  • sunglasses, sunscreen, a cap or hat;
  • water bottles or a hydration bladder (at least 2 L per day);
  • warm clothes and a windbreaker (the site is at an altitude of 2200 m);
  • thick trousers for the highline, against rubbing;
  • hiking shoes;
  • a mattress and a warm sleeping bag for the bivouac night.

How can I avoid bruises?

+–

Highlining can cause bruises: better to plan ahead so you can enjoy it fully!

We will have some protection to lend, but you can bring:

  • soft knee or ankle pads;
  • thick, soft pads (volleyball or dance style);
  • an old wetsuit to cut up (for your bottom, arms and thighs);
  • cycling shorts, leggings or tights to wear under your trousers.

How does the bivouac night work?

+–

All 5 day courses include one night of bivouac at the spot, with a highline session at sunset.

Bring a mattress and a warm sleeping bag: the site is at an altitude of 2200 m.

Getting to Les Arcs

Where does the course take place?

+–

The courses take place in the Les Arcs resort, in Savoie.

On the first day, we meet at 9 a.m. at the Slackcamp, past the “Tommelet” ski tow in Arc 1600.

Highlining takes place at the Deux Têtes spot, a 10 minute walk from the top of the Cachette chairlift, which leaves from Arc 1600.

Can I come without a car?

+–

Absolutely! The easiest way to get to Les Arcs is to take the train to Bourg Saint Maurice station.

From there, the funicular takes you straight to Arc 1600.

The Slackcamp is a 15 minute walk from the top of the funicular.

Sleeping and living on site

How do I organize my accommodation?

+–

The association runs a bivouac area called the Slackcamp. It can be reached on foot or by vehicle, and you can stay there in a tent, a van or a camper. It is a 15 minute walk from the heart of the resort and the Cachette chairlift.

This base camp has water and dry toilets, but no electricity or showers: come self sufficient (solar shower, camping stove…).

When you register, you can choose the option of sleeping at the Slackcamp. The ground slackline sessions take place at this base camp.

I would rather rent a place, where is it most convenient?

+–

If you prefer camping:

  • without a car: look at the campsites in Bourg Saint Maurice (funicular access);
  • with a car: the Lanchettes campsite is in a magnificent setting!

Otherwise, there are many Airbnbs and rentals. We advise you to look in Arc 1600 or Bourg Saint Maurice if you want to avoid commuting.

How do I organize my meals?

+–

Plan to be self sufficient for your meals.

In Bourg Saint Maurice, you will find supermarkets (Intermarché, Super U, Lidl), organic shops (Biocoop, BioMonde, La Vie Claire), bakeries and cheese shops.

We advise you to do your main shopping in the valley at the start of the week, then top up in Les Arcs if needed. There is also a bakery in Arc 1600.

Can I arrive the night before? When does the course end?

+–

Yes, you can arrive the night before.

The course starts at 9 a.m. on the first day and ends at 5 p.m. on the last day.

Companions and dogs

Can a friend or partner come with me?

+–

Yes, that is possible.

Someone can come with you to the highline spot. It can be reached by chairlift or on foot (a 1.5 to 2 hour walk).

Your companion can also stay with us at the Slackcamp by becoming a member of the association (€25).

Can I come with my dog?

+–

To reach the highline spot, we take a chairlift, on which dogs are not allowed.

If you still come with your dog, you will need to reach the spot on foot (about 600 m of elevation gain, a 1.5 to 2 hour walk).

At the Slackcamp, dogs must be kept on a leash.

To make the most of the course, we advise you to have your doggie looked after :)
